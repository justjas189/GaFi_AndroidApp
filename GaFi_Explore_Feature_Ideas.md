# GaFi — Explore Tab: Feature Design Document

**Purpose:** Fill out the Explore tab with engaging, gamified features beyond the existing three cards (Leaderboard, Achievements, Manage Friends).

**Design pillars to hit:** RPG/story immersion, customization, cooperative play, lore, and mini-economies. Avoid duplicating the analytical finance tools that already live in the Expenses and Predictions tabs.

**Core design principle:** Every cosmetic, currency, or reward must be earned through *real* financial behavior — logging expenses, staying under budget, hitting savings goals, maintaining streaks. The fun is the wrapper; the financial habit is the engine.

---

## 1. Koin's Boutique

**Subtitle:** *Dress up and decorate your AI buddy.*

**Gameplay loop:**
- You earn a soft currency — call them **Sprouts** (or reuse your existing coin system) — by completing daily financial tasks, beating weekly budgets, and hitting savings milestones.
- Spend Sprouts in the Boutique on cosmetics for Koin: hats, outfits, seasonal costumes, accessories, even animated emotes and idle animations.
- Unlocked items change how Koin appears on the Story Mode map and inside the chat screen.
- Rare/legendary cosmetics gate behind *behavioral* achievements ("Stay under budget 30 days straight"), not just raw spending of currency.

**Why it makes money management fun:**
People bond with characters they can shape. Once a user has personalized Koin, that mascot becomes *theirs* — and abandoning the app means abandoning a buddy they invested in. It converts dry budgeting into a steady drip of cosmetic rewards, and it gives long-term users something to chase well after the novelty of basic budgeting wears off.

---

## 2. Savings Guilds (Guild Hall)

**Subtitle:** *Team up. Save together. Win together.*

**Gameplay loop:**
- Players form or join a small **Guild** (4–8 friends, or matchmade strangers).
- Each week the Guild gets a shared **collective quest** — e.g., "Together, log 100 expenses" or "Combined, save ₱10,000 this week."
- Every member's real financial actions contribute to the shared progress bar. Slacking is visible to teammates → soft social accountability.
- Completing a Guild quest unlocks a **Guild Buff** for everyone (e.g., +20% Sprouts for the next week) plus exclusive guild-only cosmetics.
- A **Guild Leaderboard** ranks guilds against each other for a seasonal trophy.

**Why it makes money management fun:**
Saving is lonely and easy to quit alone. Co-op turns it social: your friends are *counting on you* to log that expense or skip that impulse buy. Peer pressure becomes a feature. It also creates natural word-of-mouth growth — players recruit friends to fill their guild.

---

## 3. The Chronicle (Lore Codex)

**Subtitle:** *Uncover the secrets of the GaFi world.*

**Gameplay loop:**
- A collectible, unlockable storybook of the GaFi universe — Koin's origin, the lore of the map regions, the NPCs you meet in Story Mode, the mythology of the world's economy.
- Each entry is locked behind a financial milestone: hit a savings goal → unlock a chapter; complete a budgeting streak → reveal an NPC's backstory; finish a Story Mode level → open a new region's history.
- Some entries are **fragmented** — you collect lore "pages" scattered as rewards, and assembling a full set unlocks a hidden cinematic or a secret Koin emote.

**Why it makes money management fun:**
It reframes progress as *discovery*. Instead of "I logged my spending," it's "I unlocked the next chapter of the story." Curiosity is one of the strongest retention drivers — players keep going to find out what happens next, and the lore quietly rewards the exact behaviors you want to reinforce.

---

## 4. Seasonal Sagas (Limited-Time Events)

**Subtitle:** *New challenges every season. Rewards that won't return.*

**Gameplay loop:**
- Rotating, time-limited event chapters with their own theme, art, story, and exclusive rewards — e.g., "The Frost Festival" (holiday over-spending survival), "Harvest of Thrift," "The Great Reset" (New Year goal-setting).
- Each event has a themed quest track with milestones tied to real money habits during that period (notoriously hard ones like holidays, paydays, sales).
- Rewards are **exclusive and never re-released**: limited cosmetics, a unique Koin costume, an event-only Chronicle chapter, a profile badge.

**Why it makes money management fun:**
Seasonal events create urgency and a reason to return *right now*. They also let you target the exact moments people overspend (holidays, sales, paydays) with a fun framing instead of a guilt-trip notification. The "never again" exclusivity drives both engagement and a small dose of healthy FOMO that brings lapsed users back.

---

## 5. The Trading Post (Mini-Economy & Power-Ups)

**Subtitle:** *Spend your Sprouts wisely, adventurer.*

**Gameplay loop:**
- A rotating shop of **consumable power-ups** purchased with earned currency — strategic resources, not just cosmetics:
  - **Streak Shield** — protect your logging/budget streak for one missed day.
  - **Double Sprouts Token** — 2× currency for 24 hours.
  - **XP Booster** — accelerate rank progression for a set time.
  - **Quest Reroll** — swap out a daily task you can't complete.
- Stock refreshes daily/weekly, creating a "check back to see what's available" habit.
- Pricing forces real decisions: do I save my Sprouts for that legendary Boutique outfit, or spend now on a Streak Shield to protect my run?

**Why it makes money management fun:**
This is the cleverest pillar — it teaches *budgeting through the game's own economy*. Deciding how to spend limited Sprouts mirrors deciding how to spend limited money. Players practice trade-offs and delayed gratification on in-game currency, building the exact mental muscle they need for real finances — without it feeling like a lesson.

---

## Bonus Idea: The Hideout

**Subtitle:** *Build and decorate your home base on the map.*

**Gameplay loop:**
- A personal space on the Story Mode map that the player decorates with furniture, trophies, and themed sets bought with Sprouts or earned via milestones.
- Achievements and savings goals manifest physically — hit a goal, earn a trophy that sits on a shelf; complete a Saga, unlock a themed room.
- Friends can **visit** each other's Hideouts (ties into Manage Friends), turning your progress into something you show off.

**Why it makes money management fun:**
A persistent, visible home turns abstract financial progress into a tangible, growing space. It's a long-horizon goal that never "completes," and the social visit mechanic adds a flex factor that motivates continued saving.

## Summary Table

| Feature | Pillar | Retention Driver |
|---|---|---|
| Koin's Boutique | Customization | Emotional attachment, endless cosmetic chase |
| Savings Guilds | Co-op | Social accountability, viral recruitment |
| The Chronicle | Lore / Story | Curiosity, "what happens next" |
| Seasonal Sagas | Events | Urgency, exclusivity, FOMO |
| The Trading Post | Mini-economy | Strategic trade-offs, daily check-ins |
| The Hideout (bonus) | Customization / Social | Long-horizon goal, social flex |

---

## Recommended Build Order (suggestion)

1. **Koin's Boutique** + a shared currency system first — the currency it introduces becomes the backbone for every other feature.
2. **The Trading Post** next — reuses the same currency, low art cost, high engagement.
3. **The Chronicle** — content-driven, scales with your Story Mode work.
4. **Savings Guilds** — highest backend/social complexity, build once the core economy is proven.
5. **Seasonal Sagas** and **The Hideout** as ongoing live-ops content.

*The currency system is the keystone. Decide its name, earn rates, and sinks before building any single card, because four of the six features depend on it.*
